Swimcraft

Turn your favourite swims into virtual fishing venues

Fish the demo before anyone else does.

Swimcraft is the fishing simulator I've been building — photograph a water you know, paint its depths, stock it, and fish it properly. The demo opens on Steam on 25 September. This is that demo, three weeks early, going to a small number of anglers.

What you catch is going to stay caught. The records database was wiped clean on 6 September and the boards are empty. Every record set from here stands — so the names on the world, national, venue and peg boards when the first Steam player arrives will be the names of the people who fish it this month. There is no second wipe planned unless something major comes up during your testing.

Three venues, two guided walks, real tackle where the rig actually matters, and the venue creator at level five. It takes an evening. It'll take more if you're the sort who wants the specimen.

Read the Tester Pack on that page first. Ten minutes, and it covers the install, which venue to start on, and where the demo ends.

Windows will warn you. A blue box saying it doesn't recognise the file. Click More info, then Run anyway — the build isn't code-signed yet, that's all it means. There's no installer: unzip it and run Swimcraft.exe.

Make a fresh account. The wipe took the old ones with it. Use a real email address; password recovery goes through it.

Please don't pass the build on. If you know somebody who'd be good on it, send their name rather than the file.

Anything at all back to swimcraftfishing@gmail.com. The most useful thing you can send is the moment you stopped and thought what am I supposed to do now? — even if you worked it out ten seconds later.
