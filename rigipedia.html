Rigipedia

Every rig in Swimcraft, one by one: what it is, how it fishes, what it is good for and when to reach for something else. Pick a rig in the water below, or scroll through.

How much your rig matters in the demo. The right rig gives you a small nudge, not a big one. Everything about your rig together can change a fish's chance of biting by 4% at most, and usually by less. It's kept deliberately subtle so the fishing stays fair while we tune it, and we plan to make rig choice count for more in Early Access.

The descriptions are how each rig works on real water. The line marked In Swimcraft is what it does in the game today.

Float rigs

How they fish in Swimcraft

On a float, the part of your rig that counts is which float you pair with which bait. The line under each float below says how. Your hook, line and bait still matter as they always have.

Waggler

A float fixed to the line at its base only, locked in place with shot.

Fishing
Coarse
Method
Float
In the game

The waggler is the everyday float for still and slow water. Because only the bottom is attached, you can sink the line between rod and float so wind and surface drift don't drag the bait off course.

Good for

  • Still and slow-moving water
  • Casting a good distance
  • Windy days, with the line sunk

Less good for

  • Fast rivers, where it drags the bait
  • Holding a bait back against the flow

In Swimcraft A slight edge with corn, pellets, hemp and tiger nuts, and natural baits like maggots, worms and bread; slightly fewer bites with boilies, pop-ups and wafters.

Feeder and ledger rigs

Cage Method Inline Maggot Running bomb Inline lead Flat pear

How they fish in Swimcraft

Three things count on a feeder or lead: the rig against your bait (under each rig below), the weight against the size of fish you're after, and your hooklength against the bottom.

Weight against fish size

  • Fish under 4lb: slight edge on 20g and 30g; slightly fewer bites on 60g, 80g and 3oz
  • Fish 4 to 10lb: slight edge on 45g and 2oz; slightly fewer bites on 3oz
  • Fish 10 to 20lb: slight edge on 60g and 80g
  • Fish 20lb and over: slight edge on 80g and 3oz

Hooklength against the bottom

  • Short, under 4 inches: slight edge over gravel, sand and clay; slightly fewer bites over silt / mud, rocks and weed
  • Medium, 4 to 8 inches: slight edge over sand; slightly fewer bites over rocks
  • Long, over 8 inches: slight edge over silt / mud, rocks and weed; slightly fewer bites over gravel, sand and clay

Weed and the bottom under it both count, so a long hooklink over weedy silt gets both edges.

Carp rigs

Hair rig Blowback Spinner rig Combi rig KD rig Chod rig Multi rig

How they fish in Swimcraft

Carp rigs have the most going on: the rig against your bait (under each rig below), the hooklink material and the hook you pair it with, and your hooklength against the bottom, which works the same way as on a feeder. Carp rigs have no weight setting, so weight doesn't count here.

Hooklink material: Coated braid and fluorocarbon give a slight edge on their own; stiff fluoro boom gives slightly fewer bites on its own. Some pairings with a hook pattern add a little more:

  • Coated braid: slight edge with Wide Gape and Long Shank hooks; slightly fewer bites with Chod Point hooks
  • Fluorocarbon: slight edge with Curved Shank and Long Shank hooks
  • Stiff fluoro boom: slight edge with Curved Shank and Chod Point hooks
  • Soft braid: slight edge with Wide Gape hooks; slightly fewer bites with Chod Point hooks
  • Mono: slight edge with Wide Gape hooks

The hair rig changed carp fishing. With the bait off the hook, the hook point is free, so when a carp sucks the bait in the hook goes in with it and catches in the bottom lip.

Good for

  • Boilies and other bottom baits
  • A simple, reliable all-rounder

Less good for

In Swimcraft A slight edge with boilies; slightly fewer bites with pop-ups. Other baits fish as normal.

Pulley rig

Fishing
Sea
Method
Sea ledger
In the game

When a fish takes, it pulls the lead up towards the bead and off the bottom, which helps it clear rocks and weed rather than snagging. It also clips down for a clean cast.

Good for

  • Rough ground
  • Bigger fish like cod, bass and conger

Less good for

  • Small fish
  • Presenting more than one bait
