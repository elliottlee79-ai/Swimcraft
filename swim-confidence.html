SWIM Confidence

The bar above your feed bar, what fills it, and what it is worth.

Last updated: November 2026

Every angler knows the feeling of a swim switching on: an hour of nothing, then small fish, then better fish, then the one you came for. Swimcraft models that directly. The bar marked Swim shows how switched on the fish are at the spot your bait is fishing, and it is the biggest thing standing between you and a big fish.

What the bar is telling you

It runs from cold to switched on, and it names where it is:

  • quietNothing happening yet. This is where every spot starts.
  • buildingBait is down and something is taking notice.
  • on the feedFish are competing. Bites come more freely and the stamp starts to lift.
  • confidentA properly primed spot. The better fish are now genuinely on the cards.
  • frenzyAs hot as it gets. This is where the biggest fish, and the rewards, live.
It belongs to the spot, not to you. A spot is about ten metres across. Cast well away from where you have been feeding and you start cold again, and a new swim also brings its own mood for the day. An hour of patient baiting is an hour invested here, which is exactly the decision a real angler makes when they think about moving.

The bar climbs to what your spot is worth

The bar does not simply count your bait or your fish. Every few seconds the game asks how well the fish here would take what you are offering: the spot, the depth, your bait, your rig and the feed you have put in, in today's weather. The bar then climbs steadily towards that answer.

  • A good setup on a good spot reaches the top. From cold, that takes about 12 minutes on an Easy water, 16 on Intermediate and 20 on Hard. The climb is slow at first, quickest in the middle, and slow again near the top, like fish arriving and starting to compete.
  • A poor setup stops short. A setup the fish here half want only ever reaches half the bar. If the bar will not climb, change something: the bait, the depth, the spot or the feed.
  • Feed is part of what the spot is worth. Bait in the water draws fish in and keeps them there. Each throw makes the fish back off for a few seconds, then the climb carries on.
  • Run the feed bar dry and the swim cools quickly. With nothing left in the water, a full bar empties in about 11 minutes on Easy, 15 on Intermediate and 19 on Hard. With feed still in, it falls far more slowly.

How long feed lasts

Feed the fish are not eating breaks down slowly, over about 20 minutes, sooner or later depending on the bait. Feed the fish are eating goes much faster: a spot feeding as hard as the water allows clears a full bar in about a minute. So the better the fishing gets, the faster your bait goes, and the harder you have to work to keep it there. At sea, chum is a slow-release slick and lasts longer.

But do not pile it in, and the bar tells you

You cannot overfill the swim by accident, because the feed bar warns you first. It reads consuming in green while there is room, swim fed in amber as it fills, and one more spooks it in red when there is no room left for another throw. The red band means exactly what it says, every time.

From an empty bar that is three throws to reach red and a fourth to ruin it. Throw on a red bar and the feed goes backwards, the swim loses four tenths of its confidence, and it takes about two minutes to settle before it will fish properly again. Keep throwing on red and it keeps costing you. Little and often is the answer, and the bar says so.

What moves the bar

What happensEffect
A setup the fish want, with feed inA steady climb towards what the spot is worth
Landing a fishA step up, bigger for a bigger fish, which eases back towards what the spot is worth
The feed bar running emptyDrains the bar, faster than it climbs
Fumbling a take: striking early, missingA sting. Recoverable.
Losing a hooked fishBy far the biggest single hit in the game
Over-feeding: throwing on a red barFour tenths off, every time you do it
Landing a specimenSpends some of it back, deliberately (see below)
Losing a fish is the one to take seriously. A lost two-pounder takes about three quarters of a full bar, and a bigger fish can take the lot. Fumbling the strike costs about a seventh. It is worth playing a hooked fish patiently rather than bullying it, not because the fish is precious, but because losing it takes the swim down with it.

Why landing a monster knocks the bar down

That dip is intended, and it is not a punishment. A big capture settles a swim: the commotion, the fish gone from the spot. The bigger the fish, the bigger the dip, and a real monster can take the bar most of the way back to quiet, so monsters cannot be chained. You rebuild, and you earn the next one.

What a hot swim actually buys

Two things, and the second is the important one:

  • A gentle tilt in stamp. As confidence builds, the fish you hook trend bigger within what the venue holds. It is a nudge, not a switch: small fish never stop showing up.
  • Whether a specimen will take at all. This is the big one. Confidence counts for more than anything else in whether the venue's proper fish even considers your bait. Conditions and gear lend a little on top, but only a little: good bait alone cannot make a hard fish easy. A big fish from a cold start needs a primed swim or a red-letter day.

Cold water makes fishing harder overall, but it makes the big ones braver: winter is slow and its fish are large. And every session carries a mood you cannot see: some days it do, some days it don't. That is the game being honest about fishing, not the game being unfair.

Tokens come off a well-built swim

A specimen is a fish of at least a quarter of its national record, or any named fish. Land one while the Swim bar is at least half full and it may drop a token. Below half the bar, a specimen never drops one.

  • XP boosts. One specimen in four drops a boost, which earns you extra XP on every fish for one hour. How big a boost can be depends on the water: +10% on Easy, up to +35% on Intermediate and up to +50% on Hard. A named fish always drops +50%.
  • Venue tokens. About one specimen in thirty drops a venue token, twice as often on Hard waters and five times as often for a named fish, up to three a day. A venue token lets you build a water of your own and send it in to be published.

Each token gets its own card after the catch card. Use a boost there and then, or keep it for a session that counts: kept tokens wait in the Lodge, under Tokens. That gives the whole system a point: the swim you patiently built is the swim that pays you.

How to fish it, in practice

  1. Pick your swim and commit to it. Confidence does not travel. Moving every ten minutes, or casting somewhere new each time, means starting from cold every time.
  2. Get the setup right. If the bar stops climbing, the fish are telling you something. Try another bait, depth or spot.
  3. Bait first, and keep baiting. Feed where your hookbait lies, then top it up little and often. Watch the feed bar and never throw on red.
  4. Take the small fish. They are not a waste of the swim; each one lifts it. Land everything.
  5. Play hooked fish properly. One lost fish undoes a long stretch of patient work.
  6. When it reaches the top, stay there. Keep feeding, fish your best bait and spot, and that is when the specimen, and the token, arrives.
  7. After a big one, expect the dip and start rebuilding straight away rather than moving.
The short version. One swim. The setup the fish want. Bait little and often, never on red. Land the little ones, don't lose the big ones, and everything else follows.
